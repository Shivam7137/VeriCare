## 2. The ED journey and metric definitions

### Steps (generic Canadian flow, with NS specifics)

Figure 3 of the Ontario Auditor General's report gives the canonical flow (CONFIRMED): arrival by ambulance or walk-in, triage and registration, nurse assessment, physician initial assessment, care and treatment, with diagnostics and consultation services in parallel, then discharge or admission. "While waiting for a physician initial assessment, a nurse may request early testing (such as blood work)" under medical directives.

The Health Transformation Hub ED Access page redirects to a login and was not read.

## 5. Arrival and capacity patterns

No Canadian or NS hourly curve with numbers was retrievable.

- NHS England A&E: highest attendances each day are 11:00-12:00; the early hours are lowest. CONFIRMED (NHS time-of-day page, https://nhsd-proxy.openprescribing.net/data-and-information/publications/statistical/hospital-accident--emergency-activity/2020-21/time-of-day). It is a UK proxy.
- Canadian paediatric ED (BC Children's, 2015-16): 12% overnight (00-08), 42% day (08-16), 45% evening (16-24). SECONDARY.
- Thunder Bay ED (CJEM): acute-care arrivals follow essentially the same daily pattern each day, while fast-track differs by weekday and weekend; Poisson GAM modelling. Hourly values not in the abstract. CONFIRMED (abstract only). https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-emergency-medicine/article/developing-emergency-department-physician-shift-schedules-optimized-to-meet-patient-demand/11F588733ADA00A346F2C21DA02E1A52
- Physician throughput: no sourced patients-per-physician-hour. An Alberta study finds time since shift start is the strongest predictor of productivity (SECONDARY, https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/msom.2023.0081). The BC staffing model allocated 1 FTE per 3,000 patients a year in high-acuity EDs (SECONDARY). Use the INFERRED values in section 7.
- Time-varying physician productivity is a known effect (the MSOM paper above). Waits are shortest for morning arrivals in NHS data (Rosenbaum, SECONDARY).

## 9. Design implications for VeriCare

1. **Define the clock the way NS and CIHI do.** NS DHW counts triage to "clinician" (includes NPs and PAs, and RAZ); CIHI counts the earlier of triage or registration to physician. The family-facing "seen by" event must map to the first of {physician, NP, PA} in OPOR `participant[]`, and the estimate clock must start at triage. Display door-to-triage separately (median about 13 min).
2. **Waiting is the majority of the experience and is dominated by CTAS 3-5.** With about 65% of patients in CTAS 1-3 and an NS average wait of 2.96 h, the demo's "CTAS 4: 1 to 3 hours" range is plausible only if the sim's queue reproduces long tails; use the NS-calibrated column (CTAS 4 median about 2.75 h, p90 about 8 h). Our earlier "about 1-3 hours" example is on the optimistic side for NS-like conditions; consider "2 to 5 hours" for a busy-hour demo.
3. **Tests run in parallel with waiting.** Waiting Room Care Providers start bloodwork, ECG and X-ray before the physician (CONFIRMED). The journey cannot be a strict five-step line; a patient can be "waiting to be seen" and "tests in progress" at once. Model status as a primary step plus a set of parallel flags.
4. **Reassessment events are the best "still being looked after" signal.** WRCPs reassess by CTAS interval and escalate. If OPOR records those as documentation events, VeriCare can say "your nurse rechecked you at 14:10" without any clinical content. Treat as a pilot ask, not a promise.
5. **RAZ and split flow create a second path.** Mobile, bed-free patients go through a Rapid Assessment Zone (up to 30% at Halifax Infirmary, 8-16 h/day). The estimate engine should treat RAZ and main-ED patients as separate queues, or waits for CTAS 3-4 will be overpredicted at those hours.
6. **LWBS is a 10% problem that is concentrated in CTAS 3.** Nearly half of NS LWBS patients were CTAS 3 (SECONDARY), who are supposed to be seen in 30 min. Keep the LEAVING flow and escalate for CTAS 2-3 first; suppress estimates for CTAS 1-2 as already designed. Measure LWBS before and after on the simulator, using elapsed-wait hazards.
7. **Do not estimate boarding.** Ontario's bed wait has a 35 h p90 and CAEP's national p90 is 48.5 h; NS has no public number. The existing rule (no estimate once admitted-waiting) is right; instead say "waiting for a bed" with the count-based, non-numeric wording, and use the C3 tile concept ("patients waiting to be admitted") as the demo's explanation.
8. **Result turnaround is a usable, honest estimate.** Lab about 20-50 min, troponin about 45 min with a repeat 1-3 h later, CT about 1.75 h median with a wide tail, consults about 1 h median. Give ranges for lab versus imaging only, as designed; add "a second blood test may be needed" for the cardiac pathway.
9. **Calibrate against NS's own public tool.** The facility page is a p70 maximum for CTAS 3-5, updated hourly. A per-patient 25-75 range must be consistent (the facility p70 should sit inside the upper part of the range for a CTAS 3-5 patient). Show coverage as "fraction of actual waits inside the range" and also "fraction under the NS-style p70".
10. **Hour-of-day matters.** Use the INFERRED hourly curve and let load, not the clock, drive the wait; the shape (late-morning peak, 04:00-05:00 trough) should make the demo's busy-hour versus quiet-hour contrast visible. Replace the curve with the NHAMCS `ARRTIME` histogram if time allows.
11. **Do not depend on MIMIC-IV-ED for the demo.** It is credentialed, does not record physician-seen, and cannot be redistributed. NHAMCS (open, with `WAITTIME`, `LOV`, `BOARDED`) is the only open per-visit file with what an estimator needs, but it is US data and must be rescaled. Synthea gives identities and conditions only.
12. **Pitch honestly about data limits.** CIHI has NS PIA coverage of only 8.4%, the public NS report gives provincial averages and no per-patient or per-CTAS waits, and per-site charts are images. The simulator is calibrated to published aggregates; the pilot's first deliverable is the real OPOR timestamp distribution by CTAS.
