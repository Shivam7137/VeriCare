Gap: I found no strong peer-reviewed Canadian ED-specific list of "top questions families call about." Questions below are INFERRED from the studies above plus vendor feature sets (all vendors converge on the same list).

**Inferred top questions (rank order I would design for):**

1. Is my person here, and are they okay to wait? (presence/location)
2. What is happening now, and what is next?
3. How long, roughly? Will it change?
4. Are tests being done, and when will I hear?
5. Can I come in / where do I wait / can I talk to someone?
6. Will they be admitted or sent home?
7. What should I do if they get worse?

**Emotional states (INFERRED from the studies above):** anxiety from not knowing, helplessness (relatives), frustration at perceived unfairness (others seen first), fear of being forgotten. Respectful communication cues that recur in vendor and study material: updates without having to ask, a named human to ask, plain words, acknowledging the wait.

## 4. Notification design

- **Frequency (INFERRED, no ED study found):** send on step change and on range change of 30+ minutes (already in design doc); cap at 1 proactive text per 30 minutes per recipient, 6 per visit; never send "no change" texts unprompted. Offer reply STATUS for on-demand.
- **Quiet hours:** an ED visit has no quiet hours for the patient, but a family member at home at 02:00 may sleep. Let each family member choose "Overnight: only important changes" (default on, 22:00-07:00): send only admit/move/discharge and delays; hold step-by-step updates for morning. INFERRED.
- **Opt-out:** STOP in English and ARRET in French must both work; also a web unsubscribe. CASL guidance for texts commonly cited: reply-STOP plus web method (York University) and honour within 10 business days but do it instantly. Whether health messages are exempt is UNCERTAIN; treat consent as explicit opt-in anyway. Sources: https://ipo.info.yorku.ca/canadian-anti-spam-legislation-casl/unsubscribe-mechanisms ; https://www.telair.net/resources/knowledge-base/guides/casl-sms-compliance/ (vendor, secondary).
- **Family vs patient wording:** patient: second person ("you"), may include range and action ("tell triage if worse"). Family: third person with patient's chosen first name, only the consented scopes, and no triage level, no reasons, no test names, no "stable/critical." State steps neutrally ("waiting to be seen", "tests underway"). Never say "serious", "urgent", "lucky", "good news". Because CTAS 1-2 and flagged patients are suppressed, a family member who receives no update can read silence as bad news; mitigate by never sending family texts to say "no information" but by always offering the same CALL option and handing off with identical wording for every handoff (per the design doc).
- **Handoff phrasing:** identical for all, no hint of why: "Please speak with the ER team." Provide a phone number and a human route. Do not say "We can't tell you because ..."
- **Escalation line on every patient message:** "Feeling worse? Tell triage right away or call 911." Family messages carry "Reply CALL to talk with a person" instead.
- **Death notification:** never by bot, never by text. Support: SPIKES teaching for ED stresses in-person delivery, no euphemisms, brief chronology, contact info (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2966572/ ; https://wikem.org/wiki/Delivering_bad_news ; AAFP https://www.aafp.org/afp/2018/0715/p99.pdf). I found no ED rule explicitly banning phone notification; the "never by bot" rule is a VeriCare design decision. The gate must treat `deceased[x]`, CTAS 1-2, any alert as silent handoff (already in design doc) and must also suppress all scheduled texts instantly.
- **Voice persona for distressed callers (INFERRED; trauma-informed principles generally):** slow pace; short sentences; say who you are and that you are an AI in the first line; name the feeling once, no repeated sympathy; offer the choice to be transferred at any time; never argue or reassure with unknowns ("I'm sure they're fine"); on silence or crying, wait and offer a human; confirm what you heard before acting.

## 5. Accessibility and plain language

WCAG 2.2 AA essentials (source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/ ; summaries at https://webaim.org/standards/wcag/wcag22):

- 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum): at least 24x24 CSS px (aim 44x44 for primary buttons).
- 3.2.6 Consistent Help (A): put "Call a person" in the same place on every screen.
- 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum): no memorizing or transcribing; allow paste and password-manager and SMS-code autofill (`autocomplete="one-time-code"`). Note this affects the one-time code step.
- Also standard: 4.5:1 text contrast, text resizable to 200%, do not use colour alone for status, visible focus, labels on every input, `aria-live="polite"` for status changes, no auto-refresh that steals focus, no timeouts without warning.
- Reading level: aim grade 6 (NS Health's own target) and test with real people, not just a formula.
- Voice and SMS: hearing loss is common among older adults (65% at 70-79) so offer SMS/web alternative to voice and ask "Would you like me to text this?" Callers using 711 relay are slow and literal: avoid timeouts and long menus. Low vision: SMS plus voice readback; web page respects OS font size.
- SMS encoding: avoid curly quotes and accents to stay in GSM-7 (160 chars). French accents push a message to UCS-2 (70 chars per segment); French templates will be 2-3 segments. INFERRED from standard SMS encoding rules.

## 6. Recommended journey timeline UI (`/patient/visit`)

Design for a phone, one column, a single status card on top, a 5-step list below. Page also readable on an ED waiting-room screen.

**Header:** "Your ER visit" (patient view) or "Sam's ER visit" (family view). Small line under: "Last updated 4:12 pm. Updates can be a few minutes behind." (Honest-latency pattern from UCLA.)

**Status card:** big label, one sentence, then the range.

| #   | Label              | One-line microcopy (patient)                                                                                                                  | Family variant                                                             | Range shown                            |
| --- | ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Checked in         | "You're registered. A nurse checks how urgent your care is."                                                                                  | "Sam is registered."                                                       | none                                   |
| 2   | Waiting to be seen | "A nurse or doctor will see you soon. Most people at your level wait about 1-3 hours from now. Sicker patients go first, so this can change." | "Sam is waiting to be seen. Usual wait: about 1-3 hours. It can change."   | yes (consent: estimates)               |
| 3   | Being seen         | "The care team is with you now. Ask them anything."                                                                                           | "Sam is with the care team."                                               | none                                   |
| 4   | Tests underway     | "You may have blood tests or scans. Results often take about 1-2 hours."                                                                      | "Sam is having tests. Results often take about 1-2 hours."                 | yes, lab vs imaging typical turnaround |
| 5   | Next steps         | "The team will talk with you about what comes next: going home, or more care."                                                                | "The team is planning Sam's next steps. Please check in with the ER desk." | none; no boarding estimate             |

Branches under step 5 (patient only unless family scope allows): "Waiting for a bed" ("The team has asked for a hospital bed. We can't predict how long. Ask your nurse for updates."), "Moved to a unit" ("You've moved to a new area. Your care team there will update you."), "Going home" ("Your nurse will go over your care and next steps with you.").

**Always visible, same place on every screen (Consistent Help):**

- Button 1: "Feeling worse? Tell the triage nurse now, or call 911."
- Button 2: "Talk to a person" (call ER desk).
- Link: "Language / Langue / Interpreter" (language switch; "Ask for a free interpreter at the desk").
- Link: "Text me this page", "Stop updates".
- Patient only: "I'm thinking of leaving" button which triggers the LEAVING flow.

**States to design:**

- Suppressed/handoff: "For an update, please speak with the ER team." + CALL button; no step list. Identical text for every handoff reason.
- Stale feed (>15 min): "We can't see live updates right now. Please ask your nurse for your status." (hide ranges)
- Range changed: highlight with "Updated: now about 2-4 hours. Earlier: 1-3 hours."
- Closed ED: a human-authored banner.
- No consent for estimates (family): omit the range row; do not show "hidden" placeholders.

## 8. Voice script lines (Alex)

- Opening: "Hi, this is Alex, an AI assistant for VeriCare. I can give you an update on an ER visit. If you'd rather talk to a person, just say 'person' at any time."
- Language: "Would you like English, French, or another language? I can connect you with a free interpreter."
- Consent verification (before anything): "To protect privacy, I'll need to check a few things first. What is the patient's full name and birth date?" then "I've sent a code to the phone number we have on file. Please read it to me when you get it."
- Not verified/neutral: "I'm not able to share anything on this call. For information about a patient, please speak with the ER team. I can give you the number."
- Status: "Here's what I can share. {n} is waiting to be seen. Most people at this stage wait about {lo} to {hi} hours from now. That can change, because sicker patients are seen first."
- Acknowledge: "I know waiting without news is hard."
- Worse: "If you or {n} feel worse, tell the triage nurse right away. In an emergency, call 911."
- Handoff (same for everyone): "For this, I'm going to connect you with the ER team. One moment. I'm staying on the line until someone picks up."
- Distress: "I'm here. Take your time. Would you like me to connect you with a person now?"
- Never say: "I'm sure they're fine." "Stable." "Critical." "Nothing to worry about." Never state a number as a promise.
- Hard-of-hearing offer: "Would you like me to text you this, so you can read it?"
- Close: "Is there anything else I can help with? You can also text STATUS to this number any time."

## 9. Design implications for VeriCare

1. Be an SMS-link-first, no-login, no-app experience (ERAdvisor, UCLA pattern) with voice as the equal path for the ~35%+ of older adults without a smartphone (UNCERTAIN; ranges 16% to 54% by survey).
2. Show the next step and a range only; always show last-updated time and a banner that data may lag.
3. Same help controls in the same place every time (WCAG 3.2.6): 911/triage, "Talk to a person", language, stop.
4. Family tier is third-person, steps only, no CTAS, no results, no severity words; range only with the `estimates` scope.
5. Silence is dangerous: any suppression produces the identical handoff text and a human route; never "no information" and never different wording for flagged patients.
6. Death or deterioration is never delivered by VeriCare; gate cancels queued texts and calls on any such signal.
7. Language: launch English plus French (3.6% mother tongue, much higher in Halifax-area immigrants), then Arabic, Mandarin, Mi'kmaw as human-translated, reviewed sets; interim: "Ask for a free interpreter" on every screen and call (NS Health service confirmed). Machine translation of health status is not recommended without review (INFERRED).
8. Test with grade-6 readability and with real users including 70+, 711 relay and screen reader users before pilot. Record accuracy of ranges and publish coverage.
9. Measure: calls to ED desk per patient, LWBS rate, range coverage, opt-out rate, family satisfaction. No competitor reports numbers; that is a differentiator.
10. Honour opt-out in English and French keywords, and by web, immediately.
