# Stage-time model card (stage-gbq-1)

Generated by `python -m vericare_ml.model_card`. Do not edit by hand.

## What it predicts

For every open emergency visit, the minutes from now until each milestone still ahead, as a low / typical / high range (10th, 50th, 90th percentile, then calibrated so the range holds about 80% of the time):

| Stage | Milestone in the feed | Applies while |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival to triage | `triaged` | checked in, not yet triaged |
| Wait to be seen | `provider_assigned` | waiting |
| Test results back | last `result_available` among orders open now | any order is open |
| Decision on next steps | `discharged` or `admit_decision` | waiting or being seen |
| Inpatient bed | `moved_to_unit` | admitted, waiting for a bed |

Method: one gradient-boosted quantile model per stage and percentile (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`, quantile loss), 47 inputs that are all computable from the OPOR feed, then conformalized quantile regression per triage band and time of day. The API evaluates the exported trees itself; no model runtime is needed.

## What it was trained on

No public dataset holds both step-by-step timestamps and the live queue of a Canadian emergency department. The model combines three kinds of public evidence, each used only for what it can support.

| Source | Records | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NHAMCS emergency department public-use files 2018-2022 (US CDC / National Center for Health Statistics) | 86,864 real visits, 50,258 usable | Who arrives (age, triage level, arrival mode, hour, weekday), what is ordered, how the visit ends, and the real time from first provider contact to the end of the visit for each of those patients. |
| MIMIC-IV Clinical Database Demo and MIMIC-IV-ED Demo v2.2 | 200 ED stays with bloodwork | Real specimen-collected to result-available times for ED blood tests (100 patients, one Boston hospital). |
| Published Canadian figures (table below) | aggregates | Calibration targets and turnaround anchors |

**Real, per patient (NHAMCS):** triage level, age, arrival mode, which tests and imaging were ordered, whether a consult happened, whether the patient was admitted, and the minutes from first provider contact to the end of the visit (median 140, 90th percentile 390). Every simulated patient is one of these real visits, resampled.

**Measured (MIMIC-IV demo):** blood collected to result available, per test: hematology median 33 min, chemistry 62 min, troponin 58 min; a whole first panel median 66 min.

**Inferred, and cross-checked:** NHAMCS does not say when a test was ordered or resulted. Those times are placed inside each real visit using the turnaround figures, never beyond the visit's real length. As a check that the two sources agree, a quantile regression on 20,000 discharged NHAMCS visits gives the median extra minutes a visit runs when each order is present: bloodwork +72, X-ray +30, CT +67, ultrasound +64, MRI +134, consult +68 (a visit with no orders: 65).

**Simulated:** the shared queue. Waits to be seen come from a discrete-event department with triage nurses, a main stream and a rapid-assessment stream, provider and treatment-space limits, walkouts, and inpatient beds that free up mostly in the afternoon. This is the part no open data can supply.

**Crowding and time after being seen:** across 49,773 NHAMCS visits, departments with longer waits did not have longer seen-to-done times (elasticity -0.065). The simulator therefore does not stretch that time with crowding, apart from a small randomised factor so the model does not depend on it.

## Calibration to Nova Scotia

Three knobs of the reference department were tuned until it reproduced three published figures (measured over 4 runs of 150 days):

| Figure | Published | Simulated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average wait, triage to clinician | 178 min (NS 2023-24) | 179 min |
| Left without being seen | 10.0% (NS 2023-24) | 10.1% |
| Average wait for an inpatient bed | 780 min (Ontario 2022/23) | 794 min |

Not tuned, reported as a check:

| Figure | Published | Simulated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90th percentile wait to be seen over the mean | 2.18 (Ontario: 257 / 118 min) | 2.35 (420 / 179 min) |
| 90th percentile bed wait | 2,100 min (Ontario) | 1,924 min |
| Share of seen patients admitted | about 12% of visits (Canada, CIHI) | 13.8% |
| Door to triage, median | 13 min (Canadian EDs, arrival to registration) | 9 min |
| Seen to going home, median | 140 min in the source records | 131 min |
| Whole visit for patients who go home, median / 90th | no NS figure found | 5.7 h / 11.2 h |

Known mismatch: published Nova Scotia walkouts include about 11% at CTAS 2. The simulator produces almost none, because its CTAS 2 patients are seen quickly.

## Training and evaluation design

Training does not use the reference department alone. Volume (70 to 280 visits a day), acuity mix, staffing, treatment spaces, rapid-assessment share and hours, queue discipline, patience, how often tests start in the waiting room, lab and imaging speed, bed supply and surge frequency are all randomised, so the model has to learn how a queue turns into a wait instead of one department's habits. Departments are split whole: 72 for training, 25 for calibrating the ranges, the rest for testing.

Average wait across training departments ranged from 36 to 542 minutes; walkouts from 0.6% to 23.1%.

## Why gradient-boosted trees

The same training departments, the same held-out departments and the same loss were used for each model family (`python -m vericare_ml.compare_models`). Pinball loss is in minutes before any range calibration; lower is better. XGBoost and LightGBM are the same family as the shipped model and were not run separately.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Wait to be seen | Gradient-boosted trees, about ten times larger | 14.56 | 46 min | -5.7% |

A neural network and gradient-boosted trees land within a few percent of each other on every stage, and both clearly beat a linear model where the queue matters. Trees are kept because they reach the same accuracy while taking missing values as they come, needing no feature scaling to reproduce in the API, and holding up better on the small real datasets a pilot would start with. The larger tree model shows about 6% of headroom on the wait to be seen, which is small beside the uncertainty of training on a simulator.

## What these numbers do and do not show

- They show the pipeline works end to end and that the ranges are calibrated **on simulated departments built from real patients**. They are not accuracy on a Nova Scotia hospital. No Nova Scotia per-patient data was used, because none is public.
- The patient mix, orders and seen-to-done times are American (NHAMCS). Triage level is ESI there and CTAS here; the two five-level scales are treated as equivalent.
- The bed-wait stage rests on one Ontario average and 90th percentile and a simple bed-release model. It is the weakest stage and is shown to staff only.
- In the simulator a walkout's "would have been seen at" time is known, so the wait model is trained without censoring. Real feeds will not have it; the API logs those cases as censored and counts one as a miss only when the patient had already stayed past the upper bound.
- Snapshots of the same visit are correlated, so the conformal guarantee is approximate.
- First step of any pilot: run in shadow mode on the real feed, score every stage in `ed_stage_estimates`, then retrain on OPOR history with this same code.
